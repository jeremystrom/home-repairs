## Diagnosis

The concern is a hairline gap at the joint between the concrete and the brick. Over winters, water gets in, freezes, expands, and gradually opens the joint wider — eventually it can push the steps away from the house.

## Materials

- 1–2 tubes concrete/masonry sealant — flexible polyurethane like **Sikaflex 1a** or **Loctite PL S20 / PL Concrete Crack & Masonry**. Not plain silicone (won't bond to masonry well). Color: gray or limestone.
- Backer rod (1/4" or 3/8" closed-cell foam round) if the gap is wider than 1/4". Optional but recommended for any deeper gap — saves caulk and gives a proper joint profile.

## Tools

- Caulk gun
- Wire brush
- Shop vac or stiff brush
- Utility knife (to trim backer rod)
- Caulk smoothing tool, plastic spoon, or wet gloved finger

## Repair steps

1. Inspect the entire seam where stoop concrete meets brick — both sides and across the back. Note any wide spots.
2. Wire-brush loose mortar, dirt, and any old failed sealant out of the joint.
3. Vacuum or blow out the joint until clean and dry. Caulk will not bond to wet or dusty surfaces.
4. For gaps wider than 1/4": press backer rod into the joint so it sits ~1/4" below the surface.
5. Cut the tube nozzle at 45°, hole ~3/8".
6. Push a full bead of sealant into the joint, slightly overfilling.
7. Tool the surface smooth and slightly concave with a wet finger or smoothing tool — concave shape sheds water.
8. Cure 24–48 hours before rain. Polyurethane stays flexible for years and bonds to both concrete and brick.
