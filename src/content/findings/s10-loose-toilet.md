## Diagnosis

Three possible causes, in order of likelihood:

1. Closet bolts (the two bolts that hold the toilet to the flange) have loosened — easiest fix, just tighten.
2. Toilet is sitting on uneven tile and needs shims at the gap underneath.
3. Closet flange itself is broken/loose from the subfloor, **or** the wax ring has compressed and the toilet is sitting too low — both require pulling the toilet.

Start with the easiest fix. If just tightening doesn't help, pull the toilet and inspect the flange + wax ring.

## Materials

**Always have on hand if you're pulling the toilet:**

- 1 wax ring (regular or wax-free Fluidmaster Better Than Wax) — ~$5
- New pair of closet bolts — ~$3
- Pack of plastic toilet shims — ~$3
- Tube of 100% silicone caulk (white) for sealing the base — ~$5

If you're only tightening: nothing needed.

## Tools

- Adjustable wrench or socket wrench (closet bolts are usually 1/2" nuts)
- Slip-joint pliers
- Putty knife (for scraping old wax ring)
- Towel, sponge, bucket
- Hacksaw (for trimming closet bolts to length, only if pulling the toilet)
- Utility knife

## Repair steps — start with Step A

### Step A — Tighten first

1. Pop off the decorative caps at the base of the toilet (each side near the front).
2. With a wrench, gently tighten each closet bolt nut. Alternate sides, a quarter-turn at a time. **Stop** as soon as the rocking is gone — over-tightening cracks the porcelain.
3. Test by rocking the toilet. If solid, replace caps and you're done.

### Step B — Shim it (if tightening didn't help)

1. With the toilet still bolted down, push the toilet to find which side it rocks toward.
2. Slide a plastic shim into the gap until the rocking stops.
3. Snap off the excess shim with a utility knife.
4. Run a bead of 100% silicone caulk around the base, leaving a small gap at the back so any future leak will be visible.

### Step C — Pull the toilet (if A and B didn't work, or you suspect wax-ring failure)

1. Shut off the water valve behind the toilet. Flush, then sponge out the tank and bowl.
2. Disconnect the supply line from the bottom of the tank.
3. Remove the closet bolt nuts entirely.
4. Lift the toilet straight up (heavy! ~50 lbs) and set on cardboard.
5. Scrape the old wax ring off both the flange and the bottom of the toilet.
6. Inspect the closet flange — should be flush with the finished floor (tile). If the flange is broken or sits below the floor, you may need a flange extender or to call a plumber.
7. Install new closet bolts in the flange slots.
8. Set new wax ring on the flange (or on the toilet horn — either works).
9. Lower the toilet straight down onto the bolts. Press firmly to compress the wax. **Don't twist.**
10. Hand-thread the nuts, then snug with wrench alternating sides — do **not** crank.
11. Reconnect supply, turn water on, flush a few times, check for leaks at the base.
12. Caulk the base perimeter (leave a gap at the back).
